## Why it matters

The title directly occupies the broad claim “data-efficient imitation learning for robotic assembly.” It is a
novelty threat to any claim stated only as better data efficiency in assembly.

## What is verified

- Official arXiv API metadata/abstract retrieved on 2026-08-12 for `2404.03729v3`.
- arXiv identifies it as published at IROS 2024.
- The official abstract describes a small human-demonstration-budget assembly pipeline using expressive policies,
  dataset expansion, simulation-based augmentation, and locally corrective actions near high-precision bottleneck
  regions. It reports four simulated long-horizon furniture assembly tasks.

## What is not verified

- Its use of an explicit nominal geometric reference or reference-relative progress/deviation coordinates.
- It does not establish whether every appendix-level implementation detail is identical to the proposed controls.

## Rule for the draft

JUICER precludes claims that local corrective data near precision bottlenecks, simulation data expansion, or
data-efficient assembly from a small demonstration budget are new. Its method has two relevant mechanisms:

- Backward trajectory augmentation generates candidate local trajectories from demonstrations and retains candidates
  satisfying an end-effector terminal-distance test.
- `Collect-and-Infer` evaluates policies, adds **successful complete policy rollouts** to the dataset, then retrains;
  it does not describe reactive human correction from failure states or allocation over a predeclared physical
  candidate-state pool.

RFC may therefore retain only the narrow empirical hypothesis: **cross-round allocation of a fixed common correction
budget over real, safety-valid, reproducible candidate states in reference-relative coordinates**, with all
correction outcomes and safety abstentions counted. This is distinct from JUICER's augmentation and success-rollout
dataset expansion, but it is not an established novelty claim until the RFC controls demonstrate an effect.
