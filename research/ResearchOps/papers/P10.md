## Why it matters

The title directly challenges any generic claim that intervention-driven collection is new or data efficient.

## What is verified

- Official arXiv API metadata/abstract retrieved on 2026-08-12 for `2405.01472`.
- The abstract states that I-Gen autonomously generates a large set of corrective interventions with rich state-space
  coverage from a small number of human interventions, evaluates four simulated and one physical environment with
  object-pose-estimation error, and reports up to 39x robustness increase using ten human interventions.

## What is not verified

## Rule for the draft

IntervenGen precludes generic claims that a collection procedure is novel because it gets broad corrective coverage
from few human interventions. Its verified mechanism is not a candidate-budget allocator: it collects human-gated
mistake/recovery segments, repeatedly rolls out the policy closed-loop to generate new mistake states, randomly
selects a source human recovery segment, transforms it to the current object pose, interpolates to it, and replays it
open-loop. It retains only synthetic episodes that complete the task, removing the source human-recovery segment from
the retained trajectory (success-only output filtering).

I-Gen assumes Cartesian delta-pose actions, a known object-centric subtask sequence, object poses observable at each
subtask start during collection, and that a transformed source recovery remains valid under the available observation
signals. Its authors explicitly identify quasi-static rigid-body tasks and transformable valid recovery trajectories
as limitations, and list F/T sensing for contact-rich high-precision adaptation as future work.

RFC must therefore be stated, if retained, as **allocation of real correction opportunities from a fixed,
safety-valid, physically reproducible candidate pool**, not synthesis/SE(3) transformation/replay of corrective
segments. A fair direct superiority comparison would require implementing I-Gen's object-centric recovery generator
and matching its additional synthetic-rollout and data-generation resource. Otherwise, the paper must limit its claim
to selection efficiency among real correction opportunities. Reactive corrective collection remains a required
baseline. I-Gen's success-only filtering cannot be called an error: it serves a different training-data-generation
objective. Our all-attempt failure/abstention accounting is required only for evaluating an allocation rule.
