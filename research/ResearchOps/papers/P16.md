# P16

- Title: Extended Residual Learning with One-Shot Imitation Learning for Robotic Assembly in Semi-Structured Environment
- Venue/year: Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 2024
- Status: `full_xml_text_inspected`
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2024.1355170
- Claim links: `C1`

## Why it matters

It is a direct residual-assembly novelty threat: object-frame task representation, bottleneck segmentation,
one-shot teleoperation and phase-selective residual learning all overlap with possible broad C1 wording.

## What is verified

- The Frontiers XML full text was inspected on 2026-08-13.
- OEC-IRRL derives an object-embodiment-centric task representation from a single teleoperated demonstration,
  extracts a bottleneck pose through velocity/acceleration change, and transforms assembly via-points into the object
  frame.
- It uses piecewise via-point movement primitives for transfer and a residual RL policy selectively during the
  assembly phase, guided by vision/object-pose information; its reported goal is fixture-less semi-structured
  assembly with variable object locations and limited interaction.

## What it does not establish

- No fixed safety-valid, physically reproducible candidate pool was identified.
- No human corrective-trajectory allocation across rounds under a common budget was identified.
- No frozen target-weighted recovery-distribution comparison among allocation rules was identified.

## Consequence for this paper

This closes broad claims about reference/object-frame alignment, bottleneck/phase segmentation, one-shot teleop
adaptation, or selective residual learning near assembly. C1 is capability infrastructure only. RFC may be distinct
only as a narrow cross-round allocation study with matched correction resources and frozen target-weighted testing.
