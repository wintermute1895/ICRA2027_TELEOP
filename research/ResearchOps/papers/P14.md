## Direct evidence

- DPIIL estimates demonstrator-perceived environmental precision from human speed/variance under the speed-accuracy
  trade-off, estimates policy epistemic uncertainty with an ensemble, and uses their product as a collision-risk score.
- It requests expert control above a risk threshold, aggregates expert-mode states/actions, then iteratively retrains.
- It evaluates clearance-limited aperture passing, ring threading, and UR5e real-robot tasks. Its listed baselines
  include BC, DAgger, EnsembleDAgger, ThriftyDAgger and HG-DAgger.

## Consequence for this paper

- It rules out claims of being the first safe interactive imitation-learning method, first precision-aware human-query
  method, or first data-efficient clearance-limited assembly learner.
- RFC may only claim a different mechanism if shown: use a **known geometric reference** to allocate a fixed *future*
  correction budget across reference-relative recovery cells/rounds, while online safety gating is held constant across
  methods.
- A DPIIL-style precision/risk IIL baseline is required for any final RFC claim. If unavailable in the final platform,
  the paper must explicitly downgrade RFC to a protocol rather than infer superiority from comparisons to DAgger alone.
