# P24

- Title: SPARR: Simulation-based Policies with Asymmetric Real-world Residuals for Assembly
- Year: 2026
- Status: `arxiv_html_full_text_inspected; preprint`
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23253
- Claim links: `C1`, `C2`

## What is verified

- The official arXiv API abstract and HTML full text were inspected on 2026-08-13.
- SPARR pre-trains a low-dimensional state-based base policy in simulation with dense reward. In the real world, it
  conditions a visual residual RL policy on the base action and real observations.
- It collects successful base-policy trajectories under injected random residual actions to initialize a
  demonstration buffer. During RL, it adds successful trajectories faster than the current demonstration median.
- Its reported real setup uses two wrist RGB cameras, end-effector state, force and torque; it evaluates two-part
  assembly under socket displacement and pose-estimation noise.

## What it does not establish

- No human corrective trajectory source was identified.
- No fixed, physically reproducible candidate-state pool, reference-relative progress/deviation stratification, or
  cross-round allocation of a common correction budget was identified.
- Its real-world residual learner uses sparse-reward RL, rather than an RFC-style matched corrective-data allocation
  experiment.

## Consequence for this paper

SPARR closes broad C1 wording around simulation priors, vision-conditioned real residuals, successful-trajectory
buffer updates, or generic sim-to-real contact-rich assembly adaptation. B1 must remain a capability baseline.
RFC may be distinguished only as a narrow human/oracle correction allocation hypothesis, with matched-budget controls
and frozen target-weighted recovery evaluation. Do not compare an RFC human/oracle-query budget to SPARR's RL wall
time as though they were the same resource without a separate matched interaction protocol.
