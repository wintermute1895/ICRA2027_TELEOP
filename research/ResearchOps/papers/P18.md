# P18

- Title: ThriftyDAgger: Budget-Aware Novelty and Risk Gating for Interactive Imitation Learning
- Year: 2021
- Status: `CoRL_2021_Oral_and_arxiv_abstract_verified; full_text_not_inspected`
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08273
- Claim links: `C2`, `C3`

## Why it matters

This is the direct antecedent for human-budget-aware novelty/risk intervention queries. It is stronger than an
ordinary DAgger baseline and cannot be represented only by a generic uncertainty/coverage selector.

## What is verified

- Official arXiv API metadata/abstract retrieved on 2026-08-13; arXiv identifies it as a CoRL 2021 Oral.
- ThriftyDAgger learns a switching policy that requests human interventions at novel states or risky states with low
  task-completion confidence, under a desired intervention budget.
- The abstract reports simulation, a physical cable-routing experiment, and a user study. It also states deployment-
  time use of the switching policy.

## Consequence for this paper

ThriftyDAgger precludes claims that budget-aware novelty/risk gating, deciding *when* to intervene during a current
rollout, or reducing supervisor burden by such gating are new. RFC must remain a cross-round **candidate allocation**
rule: every condition uses the same online safety/intervention gate; only selection of future correction
opportunities from a fixed candidate pool differs. Add a disclosed ThriftyDAgger-style `T0` current-rollout
novelty/risk gating baseline. If RFC's gain disappears against `T0` under the shared gate, it cannot be interpreted as
better data allocation.

## What remains unverified

- Exact model, intervention duration/budget accounting, and whether any detailed mechanism resembles reference-cell
  allocation. Inspect full text before claiming a finer distinction.
